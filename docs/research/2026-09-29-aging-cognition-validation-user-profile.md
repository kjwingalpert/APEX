## Role in APEX

The Aging & Cognition Research Program at the USC Leonard D. Schaeffer Institute for Public Policy & Government Service is APEX's first validation user. The contact is one of the program's lead researchers; the person's name, pilot task, participants, and session date remain to be recorded.

The program can test APEX's research discovery, synthesis, citation verification, and researcher-trust workflow. Because it is an academic research group, its participation does not by itself validate adoption or purchasing needs among APEX's primary nonprofit and advocacy users.

## Program purpose and work

The program brings together researchers across disciplines to advance research on aging and cognitive health and support health and economics research on dementia and chronic disease. Its shared purpose is to use social science and economics to reduce the health, financial, and social toll of dementia while training a more diverse group of researchers.

Current research areas include:

- Estimating the cost of dementia through the U.S. Cost of Dementia Project.
- Comparing Medicare Advantage and traditional Medicare for people with dementia.
- Studying access to new Alzheimer's drugs, cognitive-screening policy, and clinical-trial recruitment.
- Studying caregiving and racial and ethnic differences in dementia diagnosis and care.
- Training and funding early-career researchers.

## Organizational setting

The program is one of three research programs in the Schaeffer Center for Health Policy & Economics, alongside Value of Life Sciences Innovation and Health Policy Simulation. The Center is part of the broader USC Schaeffer Institute, which also includes the Center for Civic Society, the Center for Economic and Social Research, and the Schaeffer Government Fellows Program.

The Aging & Cognition program includes:

1. Center for Advancing Sociodemographic and Economic Study of Alzheimer's and Related Dementias (CeASES ADRD).
2. USC Resource Center for Aging and Dementia Research (USC RCAD).
3. U.S. Cost of Dementia Project (USCDM).

Research crosses program boundaries. For example, USCDM shares microsimulation-modeling capabilities with the Health Policy Simulation program.

## Team and coordination

The program has approximately 20 members. Roles include co-directors, a research-program specialist, economists and professors, microsimulation modelers, a data programmer, research scientists, doctoral students, a senior partner, and close research collaborators.

Julie Zissimopoulos leads the group. The full ADRD research group meets weekly to discuss individual progress and determine next steps. Smaller subgrant and subproject teams also meet weekly. Email and meetings are the main coordination channels. Nearly every paper has multiple coauthors, and major grants are joint efforts, so most work depends on collaboration.

Relevant history supplied by the owner:

- USC RCAD has operated since 2012.
- The broader program dates to 2018, when Julie Zissimopoulos became co-director.
- CeASES ADRD began in 2020.
- The U.S. Cost of Dementia Model grant began in 2024.

## Current priorities

Short-term priorities include:

- Publishing the annual U.S. cost-of-dementia estimate; the 2026 paper is published.
- Publishing approximately 10 papers currently under review.
- Producing and publishing Cycle 2 research from the CeASES grant.
- Parkinson's-model work and POINTER economics work.

Long-term priorities include:

- Developing the U.S. Cost of Dementia Model into shared research infrastructure; current funding runs through 2029.
- Tracking the use of new and repurposed Alzheimer's drugs.
- Continuing researcher training through RCAD; current funding runs through 2028.
